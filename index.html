Laptop shortlist for local AI work

Markit Estonia (markit.eu/ee) and Spain (markit.eu/es) · captured 21-22 November 2026 · 14 options

Every option below has a 1 TB SSD or more, a portable chassis, and enough memory to run local AI models for repetitive heavy work — bulk PDF processing, data analysis, report drafting — so those jobs do not spend commercial LLM tokens. All of them run Antigravity and Codex comfortably.

What decides whether local AI actually works

  1. Memory available to the model — dedicated graphics memory (VRAM), or unified memory on Apple Silicon and AMD's Ryzen AI Max+. This, not the processor, sets which models fit: 8 GB runs 7–8B quantised, 16 GB reaches 14B, 24–32 GB unified runs 14B comfortably.
  2. Storage — 1 TB is the floor: model files are 5–20 GB each and thousands of PDFs fill a disk fast.
  3. Thermals and weight — fanless machines throttle on long batch jobs; workstations weigh more. That is the real trade-off here.

Recommendations

Best balance of weight and local AI capability — MacBook Air 15.3" M5, 24 GB / 1 TB, 1 915,16 EUR. 1.51 kg, silent, and 24 GB of unified memory is the most usable memory per euro in this list: 14B models quantised run well and it still goes in a bag every day. It is also the cheapest 24 GB option and the only one in stock (1 unit). Two caveats: no fan, so very long batch jobs slow down; and the keyboard is not declared on that listing — the part number (MDVF4Y/A) is the Spanish-market one, so ask before ordering. The Russian version is no longer needed: it costs more (1 982,15 EUR) and has a 13.6" screen.

Best if a dedicated GPU and Windows are required — ASUS ROG Zephyrus G16, RTX 5060, 32 GB / 1 TB, 2 279,49 EUR. The cheapest entry with dedicated graphics, 8 GB of VRAM for CUDA tooling, and a reasonable 1.85 kg for a 16" machine.

Best value for CPU-bound work — Acer Aspire 14 AI, 32 GB / 1 TB, 1 640,22 EUR (1.40 kg); and at the bottom of the range, Acer Aspire Go 16, 1 338,46 EUR. Both run small models on the processor. They will not replace a GPU for heavy work, but they are the cheapest way to 32 GB and 1 TB.

If sustained processing matters more than portability — Lenovo IdeaPad Pro 5 16, RTX 5050, 1.76 kg, 2 504,37 EUR.

I would leave two out: the Acer Nitro V 17 (2.70 kg, the heaviest) and the Lenovo ThinkBook 16p (1-year warranty only). And note the Lenovo Yoga Pro 7 with Ryzen AI Max+: it was listed at 2 558,19 EUR earlier in the day and now shows 3 642,95 EUR — worth re-checking, that swing is not a rounding difference.

Before ordering — five things to confirm

  1. Keyboard layout. Markit states it explicitly on the Estonian listings: International English or Swedish/Finnish on the standard part numbers (no stock at capture), and the cheaper CTO versions carry a Russian layout (in stock). The Spanish catalogue does not declare it on its own listings, so ask. Several Windows models are German.
  2. VAT. Markit is a business platform with a VAT switch in its catalogue; the figures above are as displayed with the default setting. If the university buys it, confirm the VAT treatment with Markit directly.
  3. Warranty. Apple models show 12 months; Lenovo, Acer, HP and Dell show 24 months.
  4. Which models will actually be run. 8–16 GB covers 7–8B quantised models; if 14B or larger is the plan, choose 24–32 GB.
  5. Stock and delivery. Some options ship on 24 November, others on 4 December, and quantities go as low as 2–3 units.
